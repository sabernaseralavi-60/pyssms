# pyssms: one Python package for two-dimensional time-to-collision indicators and car-following surrogate safety measures

## Abstract
Surrogate safety measures turn road-user trajectories into numbers that say how close an encounter came to a collision. Two families of them have lived in separate code: two-dimensional indicators defined for any approach angle and body shape, and one-dimensional car-following measures built on short motion stories. pyssms is a Python package that puts both on common ground. Time to collision, time advantage, T2 and time gap are read exactly from one convex conflict polygon, and ten car-following measures are implemented from the motion stories they assume. The package reads SSAM, SUMO, VISSIM, LevelX and NGSIM trajectories, smooths them, finds candidate pairs, sorts encounters into a hierarchy, names the faces and corners that touch, and ships {{bank/windows}} conflict windows cut from NGSIM. It depends on NumPy and PyYAML only. Correctness is tested rather than asserted: {{tests/total}} automated tests, agreement with the printed procedure on {{equiv/compared:,}} random encounters, and independent simulations of the motion stories. Applied to {{highd/recordings}} highD, {{exid/recordings}} exiD and {{ngsim/recordings}} NGSIM freeway recordings, the same pairs are flagged at a time to collision of 3 s or less in {{highd/m/ttc1/crit:.2f}} % or {{highd/m/ttcd/crit:.0f}} % of highD rows, depending only on the motion story chosen. All statistics are descriptive. Version {{version}} has no external users yet.

## motivation
Ask whether the car-following pairs of a freeway recording are in danger. An analyst who assumes constant speeds flags {{highd/m/ttc1/crit:.2f}} % of the sampled highD rows at a time to collision of 3 s or less; one who assumes that the leader brakes to a stop flags {{highd/m/ttcd/crit:.0f}} %. Neither has made an error: each has chosen a motion story. The number in a paper ends a chain of choices, namely a file reader, a smoothing window, a pair filter, a story and a threshold, and the chain is rarely visible. pyssms is built to make it visible.

Surrogate safety measures (SSMs) describe how close road users came to a collision, without waiting for crashes [@arun2021,@zheng2021]. Drone recordings such as highD, inD, rounD and exiD [@krajewski2018,@bock2020,@krajewski2020,@moers2022] and the NGSIM vehicle trajectories [@ngsim2017] now supply trajectories at scale, and for years the FHWA Surrogate Safety Assessment Model derived measures from simulated ones [@gettman2003]. What is missing is open, tested code that applies the published definitions to such files, so that a number in a paper can be traced to a line of code.

Two traditions need to meet in that code. Laureshyn, Svensson and Hydén [@laureshyn2010] defined time to collision (TTC), time advantage (TAdv), T2 and time gap (TG) for any approach angle and any body shape by enumerating 32 combinations of a corner of one road user and a side of the other. Naseralavi et al. [@naseralavi2025] coded ten car-following measures by the short motion story each one assumes: constant speeds, accelerations or jerks for TTC1, TTC2 and TTC3, a braking leader for TTCD, and braking of the follower for DRAC, MDRAC, DCIA, PSD, MPSD and SDI. The first is geometric and works for crossings and merges; the second is kinematic and works along a lane. Each is usually implemented, if at all, on its own and for one data set.

Open software exists next to this gap. Traffic Intelligence [@saunier] processes trajectories and motion predictions, and a vectorised two-dimensional TTC is published on GitHub [@jiao]. A light search on 2026-11-01 (not a systematic review) found no package that reads the two-dimensional indicators of the 2010 paper for any shape and angle together with the ten car-following measures and the file formats of the common data sets. pyssms is written to close that gap. It (i) reads trajectories, (ii) computes TTC, TAdv, T2 and TG exactly on one conflict polygon, (iii) computes the ten car-following measures, (iv) names the faces and corners that touch, (v) summarises encounters in a hierarchy, and (vi) ships a small bank of conflict windows for tests and teaching. Its numbers are checked against the printed procedure, against simulations and against the data sets' own columns.

## architecture
pyssms has {{modules/n}} modules in four layers (read, prepare, compute, report; {fig:arch}). Only NumPy and PyYAML are required; matplotlib is optional for plots. The code is typed and documented, and every module states its assumptions in its docstring.

The computing core is one geometric idea. Give each road user its own clock, τ₁ and τ₂. The pairs of clock readings at which the two bodies overlap form a convex polygon K, the Minkowski difference of the bodies expressed in the plane of the two clocks. Undisturbed motion is the diagonal τ₁ = τ₂. If K meets the diagonal the road users are on a collision course (state A) and TTC is where the diagonal enters K. If K misses it (state B, a crossing course), TAdv is the smallest |τ₁ − τ₂| over K, and T2 and TG are read from the same polygon as the earliest second arrivals. If K is empty within the horizon there is no overlap (state C). The 32 combinations of the 2010 appendix become one polygon, and parallel paths, standing road users and overlapping bodies need no special case. Path-time diagrams of this kind are classical in robot motion planning [@odonnell1989,@kant1986].

Four design rules follow from the opening example. Every assumption is an argument of a function (a reaction time, a deceleration, a horizon, a smoothing window), never a hidden default. A measure that does not exist for a pair is NaN, never zero: TTC is undefined unless the pair is on a collision course. Each module is checked against an independent oracle, such as a time-stepping simulation or an implementation of the printed procedure, and not against itself. Data stay outside the package, which ships only a small NGSIM bank and counts.

Software functionalities.

Readers and preparation. Trajectories are read from SSAM .trj files (reading and writing), SUMO floating-car output, VISSIM .fzp, LevelX files (inD, rounD, uniD, exiD, highD), NGSIM and generic tables into one Trajectories object. The NGSIM reader converts feet to metres and moves the reference from the front centre to the body centre. Causal or centred smoothing is a separate, explicit step.

Indicators. TTC, TAdv, T2 and TG are computed for arrays of encounters, with an 8-row form of the polygon for bodies that move along their length and a general 12-row form for any convex shape. Margins, a smallest-braking search (escape) and a disturbance set add readings of the same polygon. Delta-V and an extended Delta-V [@laureshyn2017] and injury-risk curves [@lubbe2022] give severity.

Car-following measures. TTC1, TTC2, TTC3, TTCD, DRAC, MDRAC, DCIA, PSD, MPSD and SDI are written from their stories, not copied from printed formulas. Where a printed form differs from its own derivation the differences are listed (docs/ssm1d_notes.md); reaction time and decelerations are inputs, never defaults hidden in the code.

Contact and reporting. The contact module names the face and corner that touch first on a collision course, and the pair that would touch if the road user ahead were delayed by TAdv on a crossing course; the ten corner-side configurations of Jiménez et al. [@jimenez2013] come out as classes. Further modules give observed post-encroachment time, a hierarchy of encounters, prediction-error scales, scenario summaries and comparisons, plots, and a bank of conflict windows.

Verification.

The suite has {{tests/total}} automated tests. The polygon was compared with an independent implementation of the printed 2010 procedure on {{equiv/n:,}} random encounters of cars, pedestrians, cyclists and trucks: {{equiv/compared:,}} are covered by the procedure ({{equiv/overlap_excluded:,}} start with overlapping bodies, which it does not treat), and the two disagree on {{equiv/disagree_total}} of the {{equiv/compared:,}} for TTC, TAdv, T2, TG and the first road user ({fig:verif}). With a side parallel to an axis the printed in-section test needs an exact equality of floating-point numbers; a literal implementation misses the TTC of {{equiv/axis_literal_ttc_misses:,}} of {{equiv/axis_compared:,}} such cases. The car-following measures are tested against independent time-stepping simulations of their stories. The polygon is not faster than a vectorised enumeration of the 32 combinations ({{bench/us_per:.1f}} µs against {{bench/vector32_us:.1f}} µs per encounter, a ratio of {{bench/ratio_poly_vec:.2f}}); its value is exactness and one code path for any shape.

Usage.

The listing reads one NGSIM period, smooths it, sorts all encounters into levels, loads the shipped bank, plots one window, and computes the ten car-following measures of a synthetic leader and follower. It ran in about {{listing/minutes:.0f}} minutes on the author's computer.

## examples
One encounter, four readings.

Two cars of 4.5 × 1.8 m cross at right angles at 10 m/s, 20 m and 30 m from the crossing point ({fig:poly}). The polygon K does not meet the diagonal, so the encounter is a crossing course (state B): TAdv is {{fig2/tadv:.2f}} s, the horizontal distance from K to the diagonal (panel B), and T2 is {{fig2/tau2:.3f}} s. The contact module reports that, were the first road user delayed by TAdv, the two bodies would touch at corners and not overlap (panel A). While the cars approach at constant speed TAdv stays constant and T2 counts down (panel C).

Ten car-following measures on freeway data.

We applied the ten measures to follower-leader pairs of {{highd/recordings}} highD recordings ({{highd/pair_frames:,}} pair-frames), {{exid/recordings}} exiD recordings ({{exid/pair_frames:,}}) and six NGSIM freeway periods ({{ngsim/pair_frames:,}}), sampled once per second. The leader is the vehicle ahead in the same lane. The reaction time (1 s) and both decelerations (6 m/s²) were fixed before the run; they are assumptions of the stories, not data. No outcome is used, so nothing here ranks a measure or validates it.

The choice of story decides which rows are critical ({fig:ten}). On highD {{highd/m/ttc1/crit:.2f}} % of sampled rows have TTC1 of 3 s or less but {{highd/m/ttcd/crit:.1f}} % have TTCD of 3 s or less, a ratio of {{highd/ratio_ttcd_ttc1:.0f}}; on exiD the shares are {{exid/m/ttc1/crit:.2f}} % and {{exid/m/ttcd/crit:.1f}} %, and on NGSIM {{ngsim/m/ttc1/crit:.1f}} % and {{ngsim/m/ttcd/crit:.1f}} %. TTC1 is defined for only {{highd/m/ttc1/defined:.0f}} % of highD rows, since it needs a closing pair, and TTCD for {{highd/m/ttcd/defined:.0f}} %. Parameters matter as well: with a reaction time of 1.5 s and decelerations of 4 m/s², the share of highD rows with SDI equal to one rises from {{highd/alt_sdi_crit/0:.1f}} % to {{highd/alt_sdi_crit/1:.1f}} %.

The measures also order pairs differently ({fig:rho}). The measures that include braking (TTCD, PSD, MPSD) reproduce the order of the polygon's TAdv and TG (outlined cells, Spearman ρ of 0.90 or more), whereas TTC1 and TAdv are nearly unrelated on highD (ρ = {{highd/rho/ttc1/k_tadv:+.2f}}). Where both are defined, TTC1 and the polygon TTC rank the pairs identically on highD (ρ = {{highd/rho/ttc1/k_ttc:.2f}}), but differ in value by up to {{highd/poly_ttc_maxdiff:.1f}} s (highD), {{exid/poly_ttc_maxdiff:.1f}} s (exiD) and {{ngsim/poly_ttc_maxdiff:.1f}} s (NGSIM), because TTC1 sees only the longitudinal geometry; the polygon TTC exists for {{highd/poly_share:.0f}} % of the highD rows where TTC1 does.

Two checks guard the reading. On highD the clear gap computed by pyssms equals the data set's own headway within {{highd/gap_abs_max:.3f}} m. On NGSIM it differs by up to {{ngsim/gap_abs_max:.1f}} m for a few pairs (lane changes, or the preceding-vehicle column lagging behind the positions); nothing was removed.

Hierarchy of encounters and the effect of preprocessing.

The hierarchy sorts every candidate vehicle pair of a recording into level A (by TTC), B (by TAdv) or C (no overlap), with a split by severity. We ran it on {{hier_total_recordings}} recordings of six data sets (inD, rounD, uniD, exiD, highD and ten NGSIM periods), {{hier_total_pairs:,}} candidate pairs of motor vehicles ({fig:hier}). The collision-course share runs from {{hier/highD/A:.0f}} % of pairs on highD to {{hier/NGSIM/A:.0f}} % on NGSIM, and the shape of the hierarchy differs by site: on exiD {{hier/exiD/B:.0f}} % of pairs are crossing courses against {{hier/exiD/A:.0f}} % collision courses. The counts are descriptive and say nothing about which encounters were dangerous.

Preprocessing changes the counts. NGSIM's acceleration column differs from the derivative of its speed column by {{ngsim_acc_rms/us101:.1f}} m/s² (US-101) and {{ngsim_acc_rms/i80:.1f}} m/s² (I-80) root mean square, a defect known from the literature [@thiemann2008,@montanino2015]. At Peachtree Street the share of pairs reaching TTC below 1.5 s is {{smooth/raw_ttc15:.1f}} % on the data set's own positions and speeds and {{smooth/smoothed_ttc15:.1f}} % after a causal five-frame smoothing (panel B; the two runs differ in positions and in the speed source). The package does not choose for the user: smoothing is a visible step that must be reported.

## impact
What pyssms changes for a researcher is traceability. A safety indicator computed from a drone recording passes through a reader, a preprocessing choice, a pair filter, a motion story and a threshold. In pyssms each is one visible function with stated assumptions, and the numbers in this paper were produced by scripts in the repository from the result files, not typed. The examples show why this matters: the same pairs are critical in {{highd/m/ttc1/crit:.2f}} % or {{highd/m/ttcd/crit:.0f}} % of highD rows depending on a story, and an NGSIM count moves with a smoothing window.

The package serves three uses. First, teaching and method comparison: the shipped bank of {{bank/windows}} NGSIM windows ({{bank/A}} chosen by smallest TTC, {{bank/B}} by smallest TAdv, {{bank/C}} by smallest distance; {{bank/mb:.1f}} MB) lets a student run every indicator without downloading a data set. The windows are chosen by the package's own indicators, so they are test material, not a sample of traffic: in the {{bank/A}} windows chosen by smallest TTC the smallest TTC has a median of {{bank/min_ttc_median_A:.3f}} s and never exceeds {{bank/min_ttc_max_A:.2f}} s, at centre distances of {{bank/dist_min_A:.1f}} to {{bank/dist_max_A:.1f}} m, so the rectangular bodies touch or overlap, as they do in dense freeway traffic of this data set. Second, replacing manual enumeration of corner and side combinations by one verified computation wherever the 2010 indicators are needed. Third, a common platform on which the validation of indicators against outcomes can be done: a reader, an outcome extractor (braking onset), and counts of encounters per level are in place. Such a validation was named the most critical next step of the car-following framework [@naseralavi2025]; evasive-action onset is a candidate outcome [@johnsson2022]. It has not been done here, and the examples above must not be read as one.

The software is young. Version {{version}} has no external users and no citations yet, and it is installed from its repository rather than from a package index. The shipped NGSIM windows carry the licence text of the Federal Highway Administration (a modified Creative Commons Attribution 2.0 licence); nothing from the LevelX data sets is shipped except counts and prediction-error scales. The limits that matter for use are the assumptions of the model. K assumes constant velocity and translation without rotation, so a turning vehicle is predicted along its current heading; the ten car-following measures are one-dimensional and need a leader and a follower along a lane; their parameters are assumptions; and no two-dimensional version of those ten measures exists yet. A companion methods manuscript on the geometry of K is in preparation. The package does not replace criticality metrics designed for automated driving [@westhofen2023]; it supplies the indicators of the 2010 and 2025 papers with tests.

## conclusions
pyssms brings the two-dimensional indicators of the 2010 framework and the ten car-following measures of the 2025 framework into one tested Python package that reads the files of the common data sets. Verification is part of the package: {{tests/total}} tests, agreement with the printed procedure on {{equiv/compared:,}} encounters, and simulations of the motion stories. The examples show that results depend on choices that code makes visible: the motion story, its parameters and the smoothing of positions. Everything reported is descriptive. The next steps are validation against observed outcomes, a two-dimensional version of the car-following measures, and an archived release with a persistent identifier.
